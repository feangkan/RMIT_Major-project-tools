## 2. Project summary (150 words)

**Reimagining coffee waste is not about landfill diversion alone — it is about making waste alive again, so it serves Melbourne’s civic purpose.**

Melbourne consumes an estimated **~500 million cups of coffee per year**, producing **~5,500 tonnes of spent grounds** — most still landfilled, releasing methane (City of Melbourne / industry data). Community experiments have reproduced grounds into **cup holders** and small objects. RMIT researchers have advanced coffee waste into **construction** — **Dr Rajeev Roychand** (coffee biochar concrete footpaths) and **Dr Nic Bao** (*Waste to Form* — coffee grounds as 3D-printing filament for architectural installation).

This Major Project asks the **architectural next step**: how can coffee grounds become **functional built elements** — café furniture, **shelter**, **event architecture**, **ornament**, and **facade** — through **GFRC/composite research**, computational design, and hybrid AM?

Building on my **Igor Pantic studio** (coffee carving/casting), **GFRC RA facade work**, and computational studios, the project produces a **resolved temporary civic prototype** with full drawings — demonstrating that Melbourne’s coffee culture can loop back into **spatial form**.

**AI assists** workflow; the architect leads tectonic and civic decisions.

## 3. Research questions

1. **Material:** How can coffee-waste composites and recycled plastic filaments be constrained, transformed, and fabricated into architectural components with consistent structural and environmental performance?
2. **Process:** How do algorithmic design and additive tools (including AI-assisted workflow steps) accelerate **replication and precision** without replacing architectural authorship?
3. **Urban:** Where in Melbourne can a **small move** (one pavilion typology) demonstrate **big change** in waste perception and community benefit?
4. **Architecture:** What tectonic and assembly logic resolves a **temporary, install–remove–reuse** civic object to a high level of detail?

## 4. Architectural position (well-argued)

**Thesis:** Sustainable cities are not only built from new certified materials—they can be **provisional architectures** assembled from what cities already discard. Computational design and robotic fabrication become **assistive infrastructures** that let architects work faster and more precisely with imperfect waste streams, while the **architectural proposition** (space, assembly, civic presence) remains human-led.

**Against:** Techno-utopian “full automation” or spectacle projects detached from Australian context.  
**For:** Neighbourhood-scale, demonstrable, buildable architecture aligned with circular economy and digital construction industry trends.
