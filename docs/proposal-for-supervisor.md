## Overview

This project tests whether a **self-organizing multi-agent growth process** can generate **intricate, non-repetitive** architectural detail that survives translation into a physical prototype through **additive manufacturing** and **cold-cast metal** — rather than being resolved into standardized, repeatable components.

The material stream is **recycled / off-cut metal**, referencing Fishermans Bend's identity as a former heavy-manufacturing precinct undergoing urban renewal — positioning the site's material history as the generative logic for its next architectural identity.

The project builds directly on the Tectonic Formation Lab's research trajectory — *Behavioral Formation*, the *agentBody* prototypes, and the compression of tectonics — and on my own multi-agent / LLM studio work.

---

## Position

**Intricate, not discrete.** The value of computation here is intricate, non-repetitive detail emerging from local agent behaviour — not a kit of interchangeable modules. This follows the behavioral formation argument that swarm-generated form resists hierarchical, discrete articulation.

**Why additive manufacturing.** Unlike CNC-cut or cast repeatable parts — where tooling cost is amortized by making every part identical — 3D printing lets every agent-generated joint, node, or fragment be geometrically unique at no added fabrication cost. This is the mechanism by which agent-generated intricacy can survive from algorithm to physical object.

**The pattern is not decoration on a structure — it is the structure.** Consistent with the compression-of-tectonics argument (*Composite Swarm*: foam agentBodies laminated in thin fibreglass generating both structural corrugation depth and ornamental pattern through the same local behaviour — a 1mm surface supporting a 2500mm structure), the off-cut metal connection pattern is tested as simultaneously ornamental and structurally reinforcing. Structure *conditions* the agent behaviour; it does not lead it via top-down optimization.

## Research questions

1. How does a self-organizing multi-agent growth system (cellular division / differential growth — local rules producing emergent global order) encode off-cut metal material constraints as agent parameters, rather than applying material choice after form is fixed?
2. Can additive manufacturing preserve intricate, non-repetitive agent-generated detail at joint / node scale, rather than discretizing it into repeatable parts?
3. How do recycled / off-cut metal constraints (via cold-cast bronze/aluminium and metal-filled AM) behave as agent-readable parameters — weight, flow, joint tolerance?
4. Can the connection pattern operate as both structural reinforcement and ornamental expression through the same local behavioural rule?
5. How does Fishermans Bend's manufacturing history inform the agent system's material-sourcing logic and the proposal's civic role?

## Architectural position

Off-cut and recycled metal in a manufacturing-transition precinct is not scrap to be hidden — it is the latent material memory of the site. A self-organizing multi-agent growth process negotiates this material's real behaviour to produce intricate, non-repetitive tectonic detail, where computation and small-scale AM / casting assist craft rather than replace it.

**Growth and self-organization are the same claim.** "Growth" here does not mean a biological metaphor applied to form — the tectonic order emerges from local cell-division / interaction rules, with no pre-drawn global form imposed from above (the self-organization principle common to Camazine's biological systems, Behavioral Formation, and Andrasek's growth work).

**Extending a specific precedent.** *Brass Swarm* (robotically bent brass rod) and the *RMIT Mace* (titanium SLM) both prove agentBody logic in metal — but on uniform, regular stock. Neither encodes irregular, reclaimed, or off-cut material geometry as an agent constraint. This project asks the same agentBody logic to negotiate non-standard stock — a specific extension of the existing lineage rather than a method invented from nothing.

**Lineage:** Andrasek (multi-agent / LLM pipeline) → Gibson / Wark (hybrid tectonics, discrete-element chunk logic) → Snooks / Kokkugia (agentBody, Manifold Swarm, Brass Swarm, RMIT Mace) → off-cut metal agentBody prototypes at Fishermans Bend.

## Material research (in progress)

Early testing (Pantic studio) showed bio-composite (coffee-waste) formulations to be brittle and inconsistent under load — unsuitable as a primary structural material for an intricate tectonic system. Metal better matches both the continuous, flowing surface quality the formation process aims for and the compression-of-tectonics argument:

- **Cold-cast bronze / aluminium** — fine metal powder + resin cast in silicone / plaster moulds; genuine metallic surface quality at room temperature, reusing an existing mould-making workflow.
- **Metal-filled FDM filament** (bronze / copper composite) — prints on available desktop hardware; sanded and polished to a metallic finish.

I am currently researching whether recycled metal off-cuts / swarf can be processed into a usable powder to substitute for commercial metal powder in the cast mix — closing the loop between the site's material identity and the material in hand. This research is ongoing and will refine the material approach before the concept is locked.
